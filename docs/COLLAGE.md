# The collage and its production

The picture behind the lyrics and the instrument panels is a collage of still images, and the design of that collage rests on one condition about how a viewer sees it. A viewer identifies a recognizable subject within a second or two and thereafter treats every reappearance of it as a repetition, whereas a region of texture carries no identity and can recur indefinitely. The system is arranged so that images are consumed as texture almost everywhere and as identifiable subjects rarely, and so that nothing on screen announces itself as a rectangular picture pasted onto a background. Motion follows the same rule: the camera moves in response to events in the music and settles afterwards, and no free-running periodic signal is allowed to move it, because a fixed cycle is perceived as a mechanism within seconds. The remainder of this document describes how these conditions are carried by the tools and the configuration, so that a new packet inherits them without hand editing.

## Texture and motif

The generator in `tools/make_collage.py` produces two kinds of plate from a seed, the run's plan and its note analysis. A texture layer is a large, faint image, enlarged to between one and a half and three times the height of the view and placed so that at least a set fraction of it remains visible. Only a region of the source picture is therefore on screen, and the same source appears at a different scale and position each time it is drawn. Several layers are stacked at once, and the number rises with the notes per second of the scene, so that quiet stretches carry a few layers and dense ones carry many. The final stretch of the current video is the reference for the intended density. A motif is a small cutout of the foremost subject of an image, with no outline and no mat. Each image may appear as a motif only a few times, with a minimum interval between appearances, and a motif stays out of the middle band of the view whenever the scene carries a lyric. Images are released gradually, a few at the start and the rest evenly until late in the recording, so later scenes still have material that has not been seen, and among the images available the ones used least are preferred.

## Sources and cutouts

Which pictures a packet uses, and why others were rejected, is recorded in a media manifest at `data/NAME_media.json` and executed by `tools/stage_media.py`. The tool stages each listed picture into the media folder at a bounded size and then asks `tools/subject_cutout.py` for its subject. That tool calls the macOS Vision subject lift, the same engine behind Copy Subject in Preview, and keeps the result only when the subject covers a usable share of the picture; images whose lift is unusable are named under `no_cutout` in the manifest and are then used only as texture. The manifest also lists the pictures that were examined and excluded, each with its reason, for example because a screenshot carries a caption that becomes legible when the picture is enlarged. Running the tool a second time changes nothing, and running it against an empty media folder reproduces the same files.

## Settings and where they live

The shared defaults are in `vocab/collage.json` and are validated strictly, so a misspelled key is an error and not a silent default. Choices that belong to a single packet are placed in `data/NAME_collage.json`, which is laid over the defaults and may change only keys the defaults already contain; the `quiet` windows, intervals in which no motif is drawn because a particular moment looked wrong, are an example. Plates a person has authored are kept in `data/NAME_plates.json` and are never regenerated. The default vocabulary contains no radial layouts, and the lens draws recognition squares only where an image has detected motifs, using the detector described in `docs/ANCHORS.md`. Camera modulation is defined in `vocab/matrix.json`, and `tests/test_camera_motion.py` fails if a route from a phase signal or a sine curve is ever attached to a camera parameter.

## Running the whole sequence

`tools/produce.py PACKET` runs the steps in the order their inputs require: the plan and analysis, the media manifest, the collage, and then the render, in which the anchor detector is run for the images now in use and every scene whose fingerprint has changed is rendered before the scenes are stitched. The `--small` option finishes with `tools/compress.py`, which writes a 1080p copy at a low bitrate beside the render and checks its duration against the source. Because each step is current-or-skip, the sequence may be repeated after any edit, and a change that touches few scenes costs only those scenes. Renders are large, so superseded files should be removed once a newer result replaces them; only the final video, the compressed copy and the per-scene cache are worth keeping.
