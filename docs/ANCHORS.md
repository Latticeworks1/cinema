# Image anchors

A collage plate shows a still image, and a still image has recognizable parts: a blossom, a face, a ruined facade. The anchor step finds those parts once, records them as labelled boxes in image-normalized coordinates, and lets the lens pass draw a small recognition square on each wherever the plate is on screen. The squares share the tracker's vocabulary of corner brackets, a dot running along a thin link, and a red lock, so the images and the notes read as one instrument.

## Detection

Detection is open vocabulary. The prompts that name what to look for live in `vocab/anchors.json`, with the model, the device, the thresholds and the size filters, and a packet overrides any of them under `set.anchors`. The shipped backend is Grounding DINO (`IDEA-Research/grounding-dino-base`), run through `transformers` on the Apple GPU. A second backend, OWLv2, is selectable by name. On the nine spring images Grounding DINO found the seated figures in the old photograph and labelled people as people, where OWLv2 missed the figures and called two of them children, so it is the default. Model weights are kept in the configuration's `cache_dir` and are downloaded on first use when `download` is true.

Each image gets a threshold of its own. Prompts listed under `negative_prompts` name things the image should not contain, and the highest score any of them reaches is the detector's noise floor on that image. A detection must exceed the larger of the configured `floor` and `margin` times that noise floor. A negative prompt can fire on a real object, so the floor measures what the detector claims about the image, not an independent estimate of its error; for Grounding DINO the negatives usually score nothing and the configured `floor` is what decides. Boxes are then filtered by size (`min_side`, `max_side`), by how much of the box is opaque for cutout images (`min_opaque`), merged by overlap (`nms_iou`) and capped (`max_boxes`).

## Files

The anchors folder of a packet (the `anchors` key in the packet, `<run>/anchors` when absent) holds two files per image. `NAME.detected.json` is written by the detector and records the image hash, the configuration hash, the backend, the noise floor, the threshold and the boxes; it is regenerated when the image or the configuration changes. `NAME.anchors.json` is written by a person and is never overwritten. It has three keys: `hide`, a list of box ids to drop, `rename`, a map from a box id to a new label, and `add`, a list of `{label, box}` entries for motifs the detector missed. Boxes are `[x0, y0, x1, y1]` between 0 and 1.

`packet prepare` runs `tools/anchors.py prepare` after the analysis. It detects whatever is stale for the images named by the packet's collage plates, merges the hand edits, and writes `<run>/anchors.json`, which the planner reads. The renderers never run a model.

## Drawing

Anchors are placed on the stage by the plate that carries the image: the plate's position, size, rotation and `fit` map the image box onto the flat's world coordinates, and a box whose centre falls outside the plate is dropped. The lens projects them through the camera, so a square stays on its motif through every zoom, tilt and whip. The style is the `anchors` object in `vocab/direction.json`: `cap` limits the squares on screen, strongest first; `min_px` hides squares too small to read; labels are small and appear only on squares larger than `label_min_px`; `links` joins each square to its nearest neighbours. One square is locked at a time. The register of the leading stem's newest note, read left to right across the visible squares, chooses which one, and a red link runs to it from the tracker's own point.

## Commands

`tools/anchors.py detect IMAGE...` detects and writes the sidecars. `show` prints the merged anchors of an image, `sheet` writes a contact sheet with the squares drawn, and `compare --backends owlv2,grounding_dino` writes one sheet per backend for the same images. `models` lists the cached weights with their sizes and `prune-models` deletes every cached model except the configured one.

## Collage generator

The plates that carry the images are generated by `tools/make_collage.py`; the design and its settings are described in `docs/COLLAGE.md`. The anchors step marks the motifs inside every plate large enough to show them.
